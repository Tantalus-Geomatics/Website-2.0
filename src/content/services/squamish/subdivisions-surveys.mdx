## Subdividing land in Squamish

**Who approves it.** Inside the District of Squamish boundary, subdivision applications are reviewed by an Approving Officer appointed by Council. The District's [subdivision page](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/land-development-applications/subdivision/) suggests starting with a conceptual site plan, and application steps are set out in its [Land Development Procedures Bylaw No. 2632](https://squamish.civicweb.net/filepro/documents/19302/). Land outside the boundary is in an electoral area of the Squamish-Lillooet Regional District. There, [subdivisions are approved by a provincial Approving Officer with the Ministry of Transportation and Transit](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/local-governments/planning-land-use/land-use-regulation/subdividing-land), and the [SLRD reviews referrals](https://www.slrd.bc.ca/planning-building/planning-development-services/development-applications-approvals/subdivisions-slrd). We confirm early which authority applies, because it changes the application path.

**Which bylaws apply.** The District's requirements include the [Subdivision and Development Control Bylaw No. 2649, 2018](https://squamish.civicweb.net/filepro/documents/19302/), the [Zoning Bylaw No. 2200, 2011](https://squamish.civicweb.net/filepro/documents/68132/), the [Squamish2040 Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 2500, 2017)](https://squamish.ca/projects-plans-and-initiatives/master-plans/ocp/), which sets the development permit areas, and the [Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 3184](https://squamish.civicweb.net/filepro/documents/19302/). We don't summarise them here, because they are amended from time to time. We check the current versions at the start of each project.

**The ground.** Squamish sits where the Squamish, Mamquam, Cheakamus and Stawamus rivers meet the head of Howe Sound. Parts of the valley floor are covered by the District's flood hazard mapping, which follows its [Integrated Flood Hazard Management Plan](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/integrated-flood-hazard-management-plan/), adopted in 2017. Flood Construction Levels can be looked up on the District's [map viewer](https://gis.squamish.ca/map/index.html). Neighbourhoods higher on the valley sides, such as Garibaldi Highlands and Valleycliffe, have steeper ground, exposed rock and creek ravines. Land on the [Cheekye River debris-flow fan near Brackendale](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/cheekeye-fan/) is mapped by the District as a debris-flow hazard area.

**What our survey provides.** For a Squamish subdivision, the survey usually covers:

- tying the parent parcel to provincial geodetic control, so that ground and floor elevations can be read on the same datum the District's FCL mapping uses
- locating top of bank, watercourses and natural boundaries where the District or a Qualified Environmental Professional asks for them
- showing existing buildings and their distances to existing and proposed lot lines on the conceptual site plan
- preparing the subdivision plan for the Approving Officer's review and for deposit at the Land Title Office.

Engineering, geotechnical and environmental assessments come from other qualified professionals. We supply the survey data their reports rely on.

**Older plans.** Re-establishing older boundaries in Squamish often involves two issues. On the valley floor, original survey posts are often buried under later fill, so finding them takes careful searching. Older plans also may not match what is on the ground today, such as fences, buildings or driveways. We research the plan history and reconcile it with the evidence we find before any new lot lines are laid out.
