**What our survey provides.** A subdivision survey in Squamish is the plan for new lots, a lot line adjustment, or a consolidation. Inside the District, a Council-appointed Approving Officer reviews and approves the application. A BC Land Surveyor prepares the plan. Your lawyer or notary deposits it at the Land Title Office.

**The District.** The District requires a [pre-application meeting](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/application-guides-and-forms/planning-permit-information/) before the land development application form is submitted. The checklist asks for a land title search dated within 30 days, and a site plan whose dimensions come from the survey plan. [Zoning Bylaw No. 2200, 2011](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/269651) sets the minimum area, width and depth of new lots, including bare land strata lots. In the R-1 zone those minimums are 690 square metres, 18.36 metres wide and 27.4 metres deep. The fee to subdivide into two or more lots is $1,000 a lot, with a $3,000 minimum, under [Fees and Charges Bylaw No. 3185](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/259432).

**The ground.** Squamish sits where five river systems, including the Squamish, Mamquam, Cheakamus, Cheekeye and Stawamus, come together at the head of Howe Sound. A subdivision that increases the number of parcels in a mapped floodway or debris-flow hazard area needs a Development Permit Area 2 permit under the [Official Community Plan](https://squamish.ca/projects-plans-and-initiatives/master-plans/ocp/). The [Cheekeye Fan near Brackendale](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/cheekye-fan/) is a debris-flow hazard area.
