**What our survey provides.** A topographic survey or site plan in Squamish is a drawing of the ground for a building permit, a development permit, or a design. It is not a plan deposited at the Land Title Office. It shows the ground and the features the design needs, and how those sit relative to the lot lines.

Tantalus does this work in CGVD28 unless you explicitly ask for another datum. We will adopt CGVD2013 only once it is the official vertical datum in use in British Columbia. The District's flood construction level maps are CGVD28, and the plan names the datum so the elevations can be read beside those maps. A change to the official vertical datum is anticipated in 2030. That change is on the [vertical datum](/squamish/insights/vertical-datum-modernization/) page.

**How the survey is done.** We read the current [building permit](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/building-permit/) and [development permit](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/land-development-applications/development-permits/) pages at the start of the job. We research the title and the lot, measure the site, and a BC Land Surveyor prepares the plan.

**The District.** [Building Bylaw No. 1822, 2004](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/158787) includes a survey plan prepared by a British Columbia land surveyor with the building permit application. Where the land is in the floodplain, [Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 3184, 2025](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/257860) sets the flood construction level. Your designer uses the plan to draw the project. The District uses it to read the application.

**The ground.** Squamish sits where five river systems, including the Squamish, Mamquam, Cheakamus, Cheekeye and Stawamus, come together at the head of Howe Sound. On the valley floor the plan is often about flood construction levels. Land on the [Cheekeye Fan near Brackendale](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/cheekye-fan/) is mapped by the District as a debris-flow hazard area.
