**What our survey provides.** A natural boundary survey in Squamish locates the present bank and compares it with the line on the title. The Land Act defines a natural boundary as the visible high water mark of any lake, river, stream or other body of water where the presence and action of the water are so common and usual, and so long continued in all ordinary years, as to mark on the soil of the bed of the body of water a character distinct from that of its banks, in vegetation, as well as in the nature of the soil itself.

**The District.** [Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 3184, 2025](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/257860) measures floodplain setbacks from the natural boundary: 30 metres from the Squamish, Mamquam, Cheakamus, Cheekeye and Stawamus rivers, and 15 metres from other watercourses. Where more than one setback applies, the greater one governs. Land less than 1.5 metres above the natural boundary of an adjacent watercourse is floodplain. The building inspector may require a certificate from a BC land surveyor for the floodway, the flood construction level and the setback. The District's flood construction level maps are CGVD28.

**The plan.** Fill or a wall on the foreshore is drawn as its own line. We do not draw it as a new bank. When the line on the title has to change, the Surveyor General may endorse the plan. We locate the lines. We do not decide the title.
