**What our survey provides.** A natural boundary survey in Whistler locates the present bank of a lake or a creek and compares it with the line on the title. The Land Act natural boundary is the visible high water mark of a lake, river, stream or other body of water where the presence and action of the water are so common and usual, and so long continued in all ordinary years, as to mark the soil of the bed in a way that is distinct from the banks, in vegetation and in the soil itself.

**The Resort Municipality.** [Zoning and Parking Bylaw No. 303, 2015](https://www.whistler.ca/wp-content/uploads/Zoning-_and_Parking_Bylaw_303_2015_2026-08-07.pdf) defines the high water mark in those same terms, and it includes the active floodplain. No building may be constructed, moved or extended within 7.5 metres of the high water mark of a lake, swamp or pond; within 30 metres of the high water mark of the Green River, the Cheakamus River, Alta Creek (the River of Golden Dreams), Whistler Creek, Sixteen Mile Creek, Nineteen Mile Creek or Twenty-one Mile Creek; or within 15 metres of any other nearby watercourse. Fitzsimmons Creek has its own exceptions on Schedule C. Beside Alta Lake, one minimum floor elevation in the bylaw is 640.5 metres, Geodetic Survey of Canada datum (NAD 83).

**The plan.** A building-permit site plan has to show the setbacks to that natural boundary. Where the plan includes lawfully accreted land, or a water boundary that differs from the plan on deposit, the Surveyor General may endorse a certificate if the conditions in the Land Title Act are met. We locate the lines. We do not decide the title.
