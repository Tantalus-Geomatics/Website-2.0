**What our survey provides.** A subdivision survey in Whistler is the plan for new lots. For land in the Resort Municipality, council appoints an approving officer under the Land Title Act. A BC Land Surveyor prepares the plan. Your lawyer or notary deposits it at the Land Title Office.

**The Resort Municipality.** The [subdivision page](https://www.whistler.ca/business-development/land-use-and-development/subdivision-and-strata-conversion/) says Subdivision Bylaw No. 265, 1981 explains the steps, and it asks you to book a pre-application meeting with the Approving Officer. The application includes a current title search, copies of the restrictions on title, and a sketch plan prepared by a BC Land Surveyor or a professional engineer. After the first review, the Approving Officer sends a tentative approval letter. The page says you then have 120 days to meet the conditions, and that the process can take about six months to a year, or longer.

**The lots.** [Zoning and Parking Bylaw No. 303, 2015](https://www.whistler.ca/wp-content/uploads/Zoning-_and_Parking_Bylaw_303_2015_2026-08-07.pdf) measures lot area as usable site area. That leaves out water, land within 15 metres of the high water mark of a watercourse, and land steeper than 30 percent in townhouse and apartment zones or 40 percent in duplex and detached zones. A building permit on a new parcel waits until the subdivision plan is approved and registered.
