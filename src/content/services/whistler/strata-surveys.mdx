**What our survey provides.** A strata survey in Whistler defines the strata lots and the common property. The Resort Municipality groups this work with freehold subdivision, bare land strata, building strata and strata conversion.

**Conversions.** A strata plan that includes a previously occupied building is submitted to Municipal Council. That is the approving authority named in the Strata Property Act for land in a municipality. Council may approve the plan, approve it with terms and conditions, or refuse it. Council must not approve it unless the building substantially complies with the applicable municipal bylaws and the building regulations. Council has to consider the priority of rental accommodation, the proposals for relocating occupants, the life expectancy of the building, and any projected increase in major maintenance costs.

**New buildings and bare land.** For a building that has not been previously occupied, a British Columbia land surveyor endorses the plan, certifying that the building has not been previously occupied. The endorsement is dated no more than 180 days before the plan is tendered for deposit. The Act does not assign that endorsement to the Surveyor General. A bare land strata plan needs an approving officer's approval before it is deposited. The plan is also endorsed by a British Columbia land surveyor that the buildings shown are within the external boundaries of the land, or that the easements or other interests needed for access are in place.
