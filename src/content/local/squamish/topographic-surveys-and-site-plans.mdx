**What our survey provides.** A topographic survey or site plan in Squamish is a drawing of the ground for a building permit, a development permit, or a design. It is not filed at the Land Title Office. It usually shows buildings, driveways, retaining walls, fences, visible surface utilities, and trees a designer has asked us to locate, with spot elevations and contours. It shows distances from those features to the lot lines, and to rights of way, covenants and easements on title, after the lines have been retraced. Ground and floor elevations are on CGVD28, named on the plan, so they can be set beside the District's Flood Construction Level mapping. Squamish drawings use CGVD28 until vertical datum modernization becomes official, which is expected in 2030. That change is on the [vertical datum](/squamish/insights/vertical-datum-modernization/) page. Where the District or a Qualified Environmental Professional asks for them, the plan also shows top of bank, watercourses and the present natural boundary.

**How the survey is done.** We read the current [building permit](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/building-permit/) and [development permit](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/land-development-applications/development-permits/) pages at the start of each job, because the District's submission list changes.

1. **Research.** Title, registered plan, and the charges that limit where a building can sit. We look up the parcel on the District's [flood and hazard map](https://gis.squamish.ca/map/index.html).
2. **The drawing.** We agree the features, the contour interval and the datum with you and, where there is one, with your designer.
3. **Field work.** We search for the posts that define the lot, then measure the features with GNSS, a total station, or both. On a large or heavily treed site we may add a drone flight, described on the [UAV mapping](/squamish/services/uav-mapping/) page.
4. **The plan.** We draft the site plan or plan of topography, name the datums, and a BC Land Surveyor signs it.

**When a building permit includes a survey plan.** [Building Bylaw No. 1822, 2004](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/158787) is the District's rule. The copy on the District's site is a convenience consolidation, and we read the current copy at the start of a job.

For a complex building, generally a Part 3 building, the application includes a survey plan prepared by a British Columbia land surveyor. That section does not contain a waiver.

For a simple building, generally a Part 9 building, the application includes that survey plan unless the building official waives it, in whole or in part, where conditions warrant. A waiver is not automatic for an addition or an accessory building that still needs a permit.

An accessory building under 10 square metres is outside the Building Bylaw when the bylaw's conditions are met, including the Zoning Bylaw's siting requirements. It is not outside the bylaw if it contains an unsafe condition, a hazard or a home occupation. When it is outside the bylaw, the survey-plan rules above do not apply.

The building official may, in writing, require a further up-to-date plan of survey by a British Columbia land surveyor, for the site, the finished building, an existing building, or the foundation before and after concrete. If the official issues that requirement, it has to be met.

The residential, commercial and certified-professional checklists ask, where relevant, for survey details from a BC Land Surveyor. Those are checklists. The bylaw sections above are the requirement.

**What the site plan shows.** The Building Bylaw lists the site-plan contents for a complex building and for a simple building. The building official may waive the site plan, in whole or in part, when the permit is for repair or alteration of an existing building. The lists cover bearings and dimensions from the registered plan, the legal description and streets, existing and proposed buildings and setbacks, statutory rights of way and easements, setbacks to a natural boundary and the top of bank, natural and finished grade, a floor elevation, retaining walls, steps, stairs and decks, and parking, services, curbs, sidewalks, fire-fighting access and a storm-water management plan.

The two lists are not identical. A complex-building site plan asks for the first-storey floor elevation and for grade at significant breaks in the building as well as at the corners. A simple-building site plan asks for the main-floor elevation and for grade at the building corners and at the datum determination points named in the Zoning Bylaw. Where land-use rules or provincial flood mapping set a minimum floor elevation, the plan shows the geodetic elevation of the underside of a wood floor system, or of the top of a finished concrete slab.

**During construction and at occupancy.** The Building Bylaw requires an inspection of the foundation and footing forms before concrete is poured. That inspection section does not name a form survey. A plan of survey at the foundation is a bylaw requirement when the building official requires one in writing.

The District's [Master Building Inspection List](https://squamish.ca/assets/Uploads/52ad344ada/BP-Master-Building-Inspection-List-May-2018.pdf) (last updated May 2017) asks for a form survey, a building survey at framing, and a final survey at occupancy. None of those three entries names a land surveyor. The list is a checklist. It does not, by itself, require a BC Land Surveyor for those three surveys.

At occupancy, the Building Bylaw will not issue an occupancy permit unless the owner has provided a building survey by a British Columbia land surveyor showing the building height under the District's land-use regulations, unless a building official waives that survey. The bylaw also requires as-built drawings at occupancy. That clause does not say a land surveyor has to prepare the as-built drawings.

**Flood construction levels and river setbacks.** On land in the floodplain under [Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 3184, 2025](https://squamish.civicweb.net/document/257860), a habitable area is not built, rebuilt, altered, moved or extended so that the underside of its floor system, or the top of the pad that supports it, sits lower than the flood construction level. Where more than one level applies, the highest governs.

The greater floodplain setback governs where more than one applies. The setback is 30 metres from the natural boundary of the Squamish, Mamquam, Cheakamus, Cheekeye and Stawamus rivers, and 15 metres from the natural boundary of any other watercourse, or 30 metres if the development is in a secondary floodway. The bylaw also sets setbacks from District dikes and the sea dike. No building, structure, obstruction, habitable area or fill is to be built, rebuilt, altered, moved or extended inside the floodplain setback.

The building inspector may require a certificate from a British Columbia land surveyor, at the owner's cost, for the floodway, the flood construction level and the setback. The bylaw does not make that certificate part of every permit.

The mapped flood construction levels are geodetic elevations to CGVD28 and include 0.6 metres of freeboard. The District publishes them on its [map viewer](https://gis.squamish.ca/map/index.html). Exemptions from the flood construction level are not a waiver of a survey plan under the Building Bylaw.

**Who uses the plan.** Your designer uses it to draw the project. The District uses it to read the application against the [Zoning Bylaw No. 2200, 2011](https://squamish.civicweb.net/filepro/documents/68132/), flood construction levels and development permit areas. Where the land is in the mapped floodplain, Floodplain Management Bylaw No. 3184 and Development Permit Area 2 in the [Squamish2040 Official Community Plan](https://squamish.ca/projects-plans-and-initiatives/master-plans/ocp/) are part of the review. Development Permit Area 1, Environmental Protection, is the area that usually puts a watercourse or a top of bank on the plan. A survey that posts a missing property corner is a different job, on the [boundary surveys](/squamish/services/boundary-surveys/) page.

**The ground.** Squamish sits where the Squamish, Mamquam, Cheakamus and Stawamus rivers meet the head of Howe Sound. On the valley floor the plan is often about fill and flood construction levels. Garibaldi Highlands and Valleycliffe are steeper, with rock, creek ravines and long hedges. Land on the [Cheekye River debris-flow fan near Brackendale](https://squamish.ca/building-and-land-development/home-land-and-property-development/cheekye-fan/) is mapped by the District as a debris-flow hazard area. On the valley floor, later fill can bury the original ground and the survey posts, so the search for posts is part of this survey. A site plan often locates a cedar hedge or a retaining wall as well as the house.
