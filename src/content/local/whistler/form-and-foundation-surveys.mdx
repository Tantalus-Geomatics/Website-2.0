**What our survey provides.** A form or foundation survey in Whistler shows where the forms or the concrete sit relative to the lot lines, and the elevation at the proposed top of concrete. Locating a form or a foundation against a property line, in order to certify that location in writing, is part of the practice of land surveying.

**The Resort Municipality.** [Building Regulation Bylaw No. 2482, 2025](https://www.whistler.ca/wp-content/uploads/CBYL0002482-2026-10-20-1.pdf) requires the foundation forms to be inspected after the forms are completed, the location certificate is submitted and accepted by a Building Official, and before the concrete is placed. The bylaw does not define "location certificate." The Building Official may require an up-to-date plan or survey, prepared by a Land Surveyor, that shows the proposed top of concrete before the concrete is placed. An occupancy permit will not be issued unless the owner has provided a building survey by a Land Surveyor showing the building height, size, location and elevation under the zoning bylaw. In that bylaw, a Land Surveyor is a British Columbia land surveyor, or an ASTTBC member registered in site improvement surveys.

**Elevations.** Tantalus does this work in CGVD28 unless you explicitly ask for another datum. We will adopt CGVD2013 only once it is the official vertical datum in use in British Columbia. The building bylaw asks for geodetic elevations. It does not name CGVD28 or CGVD2013. Two flood elevations in Zoning Bylaw No. 303, beside Alta Lake and in the Green Lake area, are given as Geodetic Survey of Canada datum (NAD 83).
