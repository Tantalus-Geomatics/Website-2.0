**What our survey provides.** A topographic survey or site plan in Whistler is a drawing of the ground for a building permit, a development permit, or a design. It is not a plan deposited at the Land Title Office. It shows the ground and the features the design needs, and how those sit relative to the lot lines.

Tantalus does this work in CGVD28 unless you explicitly ask for another datum. We will adopt CGVD2013 only once it is the official vertical datum in use in British Columbia. [Building Regulation Bylaw No. 2482, 2025](https://www.whistler.ca/wp-content/uploads/CBYL0002482-2026-10-20-1.pdf) asks for geodetic elevations. It does not name CGVD28 or CGVD2013. The plan names the datum so the elevations can be read beside the design.

**The Resort Municipality.** A building permit application includes a survey plan prepared by a Land Surveyor. In that bylaw, a Land Surveyor is a British Columbia land surveyor, or an ASTTBC member registered in site improvement surveys. The site plan shows setbacks to the natural boundary of a lake, swamp, pond, stream or watercourse, and natural and finished grades in geodetic elevations. Where a flood elevation applies, it shows the geodetic elevation of the underside of a wood floor or the top of a finished concrete slab. Where the parcel slopes more than 15 percent, the Building Official may ask for 0.5 metre contours.

**The ground.** Floodproofing is in [Zoning and Parking Bylaw No. 303, 2015](https://www.whistler.ca/wp-content/uploads/Zoning-_and_Parking_Bylaw_303_2015_2026-08-07.pdf), not in a separate floodplain bylaw. Setbacks are measured from the high water mark. A house on a detached or duplex lot is often exempt from a development permit, except where the lot is in an environmental development permit area and within 30 metres of the high water mark of a stream.
